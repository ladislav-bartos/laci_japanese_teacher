**Notes on the misses:**
- **はっている (#2)**, not はがしている — 虫が床をはう = "a bug crawls on the floor"; はがす means to
  peel/strip something off a surface, which needs an object being removed, not a bug moving.
- **すれちがう (#3)**, not ぶつかる — a narrow road where cars pass each other closely is hard is
  すれちがうのは大変 ("passing by each other is difficult"); ぶつかる means to actually collide, a
  different (and worse) outcome than what a narrow-road complaint usually describes.
- **曲げたり (#4)**, not ついたり — ひざを曲げたり伸ばしたりする = "bend and extend the knee," a natural
  pair with 伸ばす (extend); ひざをつく means to kneel/put your knees down, which doesn't pair with
  "extending."
- **すべる (#5)**, not はう — skating backward is 後ろにすべる練習 ("practice sliding/skating backward");
  はう means to crawl, unrelated to skating.
- **のぞいた (#6)**, not にらんだ — 箱の中に何があるかのぞいた = "peeked to see what was in the box"; にらむ
  means to glare at (with hostility), which doesn't fit curiosity about a box's contents.

This whole set is first-exposure vocabulary (physical action verbs), so a rough day here is expected —
but note the shape: every miss picked the *other* option rather than leaving it blank, suggesting the
two words in each pair aren't yet distinguished by meaning.

## 文法 — W3D3: 〜とたん(に)／〜あげく(に)／〜末(に)／〜かと思ったら — Mark: 1/5, Reorder: 0/2

### Mark ○/×

| # | Sentence | Your answer | Correct | ✅/❌ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | この薬は食べたとたんに、飲んでください。 | ○ | × | ❌ |
| 2 | その石けんを使ったとたん、手がかゆくなった。 | × | ○ | ❌ |
| 3 | 考えた末、手術しないことにしました。 | ○ | ○ | ✅ |
| 4 | 待たされたあげく、まずい料理を食べさせられた。 | × | ○ | ❌ |
| 5 | あちこちの病院へ行くあげく、やっと元気になった。 | ○ | × | ❌ |

**Notes on the misses — this is the clearest pattern of the day:**
- **#1 and #2 are mirror-image mistakes.** #1 (marked ○, should be ×) uses とたんに before an imperative
  (「〜飲んでください」) — とたん(に) can only describe something that *already happened* unexpectedly
  (a completed, non-volitional event), never a command about what to do next. #2 (marked ×, should be ○)
  is exactly the pattern とたん is built for: 使ったとたん、かゆくなった — an action followed immediately by
  an unexpected result, both in the past. You flagged the invalid "command" usage as fine and the valid
  "immediate unexpected result" usage as wrong — i.e., both judgments went in the opposite direction from
  what とたん actually requires. Worth a dedicated flashcard: **とたん(に) = Vた + [unexpected past-tense
  result], never + a command/request.**
- **#4** — 待たされたあげく、まずい料理を食べさせられた is a valid あげく sentence: after enduring something
  (being made to wait), a negative result followed (being forced to eat bad food). This is the textbook
  shape for あげく; marking it × suggests the "negative outcome after enduring something" sense of あげく
  isn't fully locked in yet.
- **#5** — あちこちの病院へ行くあげく should be 行った (past tense) — あげく always attaches to a Vた form,
  not Vる. It's also a positive outcome (やっと元気になった), which doesn't fit あげく's usual negative-result
  nuance. Two separate issues in one sentence: the tense and the tone.

### Reorder

| # | Target | Your order | Correct order | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | あの二人、さっきまで＿＿＿＿＿＿＿＿もう仲良くしている。(1 けんか 2 かと 3 思ったら 4 していた) | 1-3-2-4 | 1-4-2-3 | ❌ |

- **#6** — your order gives 「けんか思ったらかとしていた」, which doesn't parse. Correct is 1→4→2→3:
  「けんか」＋「していた」＋「かと」＋「思ったら」＝「けんかしていたかと思ったら」("just when I thought they
  were fighting..."). かと思ったら is a fixed unit (かと directly followed by 思ったら) and it has to come
  *after* the completed action it reacts to (けんかしていた), not be split apart from it.
- **#7** — your order gives 「来た遅れてあやまりもあげく」, which doesn't parse. Correct is 2→1→3→4:
  「遅れて」＋「来た」＋「あげく」＋「あやまりも」＝「遅れて来たあげく、あやまりもしなかった」("He came late,
  and on top of that, didn't even apologize"). あげく attaches directly to the Vた verb that precedes it
  (来たあげく), and あやまりも...しなかった closes the sentence as the final, worst part of the sequence —
  it can't sit in the middle.

## Summary

Today's cleanest, most useful signal is the **とたん(に) mirror-flip** in grammar #1/#2: you marked a
とたん sentence attached to a command as correct, and a とたん sentence correctly describing an immediate
unexpected result as incorrect — both judgments inverted. That's a well-defined, fixable gap (とたん
needs a completed past-tense trigger and an equally unplanned past-tense result, never an instruction),
much more specific than "grammar is shaky."

Everything else that collapsed today — vocabulary choose (1/6), reading (0/2 practice, both quiz
questions wrong), and 2 of 3 listening items — shares the Day 2 pattern of "hold the whole
sentence/exchange in mind and discriminate," as opposed to isolated recognition. Two new listening
misses in particular hinged on tracking a *reversal* mid-conversation (a proposal that gets withdrawn,
or an unavailable repair that's reframed as a consultation) rather than the plain gist — worth
specifically practicing "did the situation change back by the end?" as a listening habit.

Kanji Part A stayed near-zero as expected for brand-new kanji, but the identical wrong reading given
for two unrelated words (#6, #10 both しゅへい) is worth a note — possibly a copy/carry-over slip rather
than two independent misses.

  request, or anything volitional. Contrast directly against today's #1 and #2.
