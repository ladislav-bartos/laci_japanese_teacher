**Notes on the misses:**
- **なる (#3)**, not なった — 〜ばかりだ (the "keeps getting worse" sense) attaches to the plain
  non-past form of a verb (苦しくなるばかりだ, "just keeps getting harder"), not the past form. 財政は
  苦しくなったばかりだ would read as "the finances *just* got hard" (a different ばかり — the "just
  did X" sense), not the intended "keeps worsening" meaning.
- **からこそ (#4)**, not こそから — こそから isn't a real grammar pattern; こそ always follows the reason
  clause directly (好きだからこそ), it never precedes から. Worth double-checking word order on this
  pattern specifically.

## 文法 — Reorder — 0/2

| # | Target | Your order | Correct order | ✅/❌ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | お互いに率直に話し合ってこそ理解し合えるのです | 1→2→3→4 | 3→2→4→1 | ❌ |
| 7 | この仕事はやる気がありさえすれば、だれにでもできます | 2→1→3→4 | 2→1→4→3 | ❌ |

**#6** — the sentence is お互いに率直に**話し**（3）**合って**（2）**こそ**（4）**理解**（1）し合えるのです
("only by frankly talking things through with each other can we understand one another"). こそ has
to attach directly onto the て-form 話し合って, and 理解 needs to land right before し合える — your order
put こそ (4) at the very end instead of right after 話し合って.

**#7** — この仕事は**やる気が**（2）**あり**（1）**さえ**（4）**すれば**（3）、だれにでもできます ("as long as
you have motivation, anyone can do this job"). さえ has to sit between あり and すれば (あり＋さえ＋すれば
is the fixed 〜さえすれば skeleton) — your order had すれば before さえ, breaking that skeleton.

Both misses were word-order slips around where こそ／さえ physically attach within a fixed
skeleton (Vて＋こそ; Vます(stem)＋さえすれば) rather than not knowing the grammar point itself — the
individual pieces were right, just reassembled in the wrong spot.
