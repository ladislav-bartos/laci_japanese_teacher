**Notes on the misses:**
- **書留 (#2)**, not 貯金 — 書留 ("registered mail") is the term for mailing valuables/important
  documents securely; 貯金 means "savings" (a banking concept), which isn't a mailing method at all.
- **守り (#5)**, not 保ち — 信号を守る ("obey/follow the traffic signal") is the standard collocation;
  保つ means "maintain/keep (a state)," which doesn't take 信号 as a natural object in this sense.
- **変わり (#6)**, not 変え — this is an announcement about the number itself: 電話番号が変わりました
  ("my phone number has changed," intransitive) is the natural way to inform someone of a change to
  your own info. 変える is transitive and would need a different structure (私は電話番号を変えました).
- **決まった (#7)**, not 選んだ — the subject marked by が is 選手 (the athlete) itself, which needs an
  intransitive verb: 選手が決まった ("the athlete was decided/determined"). 選ぶ is transitive — 選手が
  選んだ would mean "the athlete chose [something]," which doesn't fit the sentence's meaning.

Three of the four misses (#5, #6, #7) are the same kind of trap: picking the *transitive* member of a
transitive/intransitive pair (保つ, 変える, 選ぶ) where the sentence structure actually calls for the
*intransitive* one (守る, 変わる, 決まる) because the grammatical subject is the thing being acted upon,
not the actor. Worth reviewing these three pairs specifically as a group.

| # | Question | Your answer | Correct | ✅/❌ |

| 1 | として／にしたら（新聞を教材） | b | a | ❌ |
| 2 | にしたら／にしたって（彼） | b | a | ❌ |
| 3 | として／としたら（旅行する） | b | b | ✅ |
| 4 | として／とした（目的） | a | b | ❌ |
| 5 | としては／としても（男に生まれた） | b | b | ✅ |

**Notes on the misses:**
- **として (#1)**, not にしたら — 新聞を教材として使う ("use the newspaper as teaching material") is the
  〜として "role/purpose" pattern (Pattern 4); にしたら ("from X's perspective," Pattern 1) doesn't fit
  a sentence about *using something as* something else.
- **にしたら (#2)**, not にしたって — にしたって is a casual variant of にしても ("even if"), which
  doesn't fit here; the sentence needs にしたら ("from his perspective") to match Pattern 1's meaning
  ("this might not be a big deal *to him*, but it's serious to me").
- **とした (#4)**, not として — when the pattern directly modifies a following noun (目的とした＋もの,
  "a thing that has X as its purpose"), the connecting form is とした, not として. として needs a verb
  after it (国際交流を目的として始められた), while とした attaches straight onto the noun it modifies.

Two of the three misses (#1, #2) were about picking the right *pattern* among near-synonyms
(にしたら vs として vs にしたって); the third (#4) was about picking the right *form* of the same
pattern depending on whether a verb or a noun follows. Worth reviewing 〜として／〜とした as a pair
specifically — same underlying pattern, different grammatical slot.
