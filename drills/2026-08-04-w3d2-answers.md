## 文法 — W3D2: わけだ／わけではない／わけがない／わけにはいかない — Choice: 2/5, Reorder: 0/2

### Choice

| # | Question | Your answer | Correct | ✅/❌ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | もう12時か。おなかがすくわけ（だ／ではない） | a | a | ✅ |
| 2 | いつも授業に来ない学生の、試験の結果がいいわけ（ではない／がない） | a | b | ❌ |
| 3 | 台風でも出かけないわけには（いかない／いけない） | a | a | ✅ |
| 4 | 毎日牛乳を飲むのは体にいいからで、特に好きなわけ（ではない／がない） | b | a | ❌ |
| 5 | 電池が入っていないから、動くわけ（ではない／がない） | a | b | ❌ |

**Notes on the misses:**
- **#2** — correct is **b** (わけがない), not ではない. "試験の結果がいいわけがない" = "there's no
  way the test results are good" (strong negative conclusion from "never attends class"); わけでは
  ない would only partially deny it ("it's not that the results are good"), which is too weak for
  what the reason (skipping class) actually implies.
- **#4** — correct is **a** (わけではない), not がない. This is a partial denial: "drinking milk
  daily isn't [entirely] because I like it, it's because it's good for me" — わけではない fits
  exactly this "not necessarily for that reason" nuance; わけがない would deny it's possible at all,
  too strong for "I drink it for health, not taste."
- **#5** — correct is **b** (わけがない), not ではない. "電池が入っていないのだから、動くわけがない"
  = "there's no way it'll work, since the battery isn't in" — a strong logical impossibility, which
  is exactly what わけがない expresses; わけではない (partial denial) doesn't fit a flat impossibility.

Pattern across #2, #4, #5: わけがない (flat impossibility/strong negative certainty) and わけでは
ない (partial denial, "not necessarily") got swapped in both directions — worth a side-by-side drill
specifically contrasting "impossible" vs. "not entirely."

### Reorder

| # | Target | Your order | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 仕事がこんなにたくさんあるんです。忙しくないわけがありません。 | 2→1→4→3 | ❌ |

- **#6** — your order gives 「忙しくわけがないありません」, which doesn't parse. Correct order is
  2→4→1→3: 「忙しく」＋「ない」＋「わけが」＋「ありません」＝「忙しくないわけがありません」("There's

  directly to 忙しく before わけが, not after it.
- **#7** — your order gives 「いくら届かない待ってもわけだ」, which doesn't parse. Correct order is
  1→4→3→2: 「いくら」＋「待っても」＋「届かない」＋「わけだ」＝「いくら待っても届かないわけだ」("No
  matter how long he waits, that's why it won't arrive" / "of course it won't arrive no matter how
  long he waits"). いくら…ても is a fixed pair that has to stay adjacent (いくら directly followed
  by the ても-clause), and わけだ closes the sentence as the conclusion, not 届かない.

## Summary

The standout shape today is Part A kanji readings: not a wall of unrelated guesses, but 7 of 14
misses sharing a fragment with the correct answer — either the first syllable landed and the rest
didn't (他人→ちにん, 分担→ぶんしん, 入荷→にゅうしん), or the ending's grammatical shape (て-form,
past tense) was guessed correctly while the verb itself was substituted with something unrelated
that happens to rhyme (凍って→もって, 届けて→うけて, 頂いた→すいた). That's a shallower miss than
"never seen this word" — the shape of the sentence and part of the reading are being processed,
just not the full word yet, which tracks with this being these kanji's first day.

Everything else that went to zero today (word-fit, reorder, listening) shares a common thread:
these are all "hold the whole sentence/exchange in mind and use context to disambiguate" tasks,
as opposed to isolated a/b recognition (which stayed reasonably solid at 2/5–6/8). The わけだ family
mix-ups (#2, #4, #5) also cluster around the same axis — わけがない (flat impossibility) vs わけでは
ない (partial denial) got crossed in both directions, suggesting the two aren't yet cleanly
separated as concepts, rather than one being weaker than the other.

**Recommend before Day 3:**
- Flashcards for today's 14 kanji (連,絡,荷,届,参,衣,他,蔵,凍,等,配,担,当,頂), with the sentence
  context included — since several misses show the grammatical shape was caught but the specific
  word wasn't, drilling the word *in context* (not just kanji↔reading pairs) may help more than
  isolated flashcards would.
- Side-by-side drill: わけがない ("no way / impossible") vs わけではない ("not necessarily / not
  entirely") — today's three grammar-choice misses all confused these two in different directions.
- 人なつっこい vs たのもしい (#7 vocab word-fit) — flagged on both Week 3 Day 1 and today; worth a
  dedicated card since it's recurring.
- For reorder questions, practice keeping fixed pairs together first (いくら…ても, ない+わけが) before
  placing the remaining pieces — both of today's reorder misses broke up a pair that needed to stay
  adjacent.
