| 2 | お話し中のところ、失礼します。 | ○ | ○ | ✅ |
| 3 | 風が強すぎて、花見のどころではなかった。 | × | × | ✅ |
| 4 | 辞書で調べるところ、漢字が間違っていた。 | × | × | ✅ |
| 5 | もっと勉強しないと、N2どころかN3も無理だ。 | ○ | ○ | ✅ |

**Note on the miss:**
- **#1** — これから ("from now on/about to") signals something that *hasn't happened yet*, but
  乗った is past tense ("already boarded") — the two contradict each other. Fixed, it should be
  これから電車に乗るところです (乗る, not 乗った): "I'm just about to board the train." 〜たところ is
  reserved for something that *just finished* (matching a word like ちょうど or たった今), not
  これから.

- **#6** — your order gives 「電話しようところと思っていた」, which doesn't parse. Correct is
  2→4→1→3: 「しよう」＋「と思って」＋「いた」＋「ところ」＝「しようと思っていたところ」("I was just
  thinking of calling"). と思っていた is a fixed chunk (と思って directly followed by いた) that
  has to stay together, with ところ closing the sentence at the very end.
- **#7** — your order gives 「テレビを見るどころあって」, which doesn't parse. Correct is
  3→4→2→1: 「あって」＋「テレビを」＋「見る」＋「どころ」＝「宿題がたくさんあってテレビを見るどころでは
  ない」("With so much homework, this is no time to be watching TV"). あって (from 宿題がたくさん
  あって, "there being a lot of homework") has to come right after the given clause, and どころ
  closes the sentence immediately before ではない — it can't sit in the middle.

## Summary

for both 面積 and 服装, and ちりゅう／ちりょう given for both 燃料 and 資料. This is now a
two-day-running signal (Day 3 had the same shape with しゅへい), which suggests something
specific: when a reading isn't confidently known, a half-remembered fragment from *elsewhere in
the same drill* is getting pattern-matched onto the new word, rather than each word being judged
independently. That's a different failure mode from "never seen this kanji before," and worth
watching for directly — if an answer feels like it might be reused from a few questions back,
that's worth a second look before finalizing it.

Everything that went to zero today (word-fit, reorder, reading, listening) again shares the
"hold the whole sentence/exchange in mind and use context to disambiguate" shape flagged on Day 2
and Day 3 — as opposed to isolated a/b recognition, which held up reasonably (Part B 7/8, choice
4/6, mark 4/5). Listening in particular had a specific, nameable pattern today: **both #2 and #3
required tracking a late reveal or an explicitly-stated real reason, arriving after a plausible
surface-level distractor earlier in the dialogue** — the same "did the situation change or get
clarified by the end?" skill that Week 3 Day 2's listening review already flagged as the thing to
practice.

**Recommend before Day 5:**
